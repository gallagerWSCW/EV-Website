Carbon Emissions trap heat and drive climate change

Why is Carbon Emission a problem in New Zealand

Climate change is a serious issue that affects everyone in New Zealand. Over the last century, the country's average temperature has risen. According to NIWA's Seven Station Temperature Series, New Zealand's annual temperature increased by 1.09°C between 1909 and 2018. One of the main causes is the large amount of carbon emissions produced in the country. In 2022, New Zealand emitted about 32.4 million tonnes of fossil CO₂, and each person produced an average of 6.31 tonnes of CO₂. This places New Zealand among the countries with relatively high carbon emissions per person.

Impacts of Carbon Emission

Rising Temperature

Increasing greenhouse gas emissions trap more heat in the atmosphere, causing global temperatures to rise. In New Zealand, this can lead to hotter temperatures, more frequent heatwaves and changes in rainfall patterns.

Sea Level Rise

As the planet warms, glaciers and ice sheets melt and oceans expand. Rising sea levels increase the risk of coastal flooding and erosion, threatening homes, businesses, roads and other infrastructure in coastal communities.

Extreme Weather

Climate change can increase the risk of some extreme weather events, including heavy rainfall, flooding and severe storms. These events can damage homes and infrastructure and disrupt people's lives and businesses.

Threats to Agriculture

Changes in temperature and rainfall can make farming more difficult. Droughts, floods and changing growing conditions can reduce crop yields, affect livestock and create challenges for New Zealand's agricultural industry.


Transport: A Major Source of Emissions

Transport is responsible for a significant share of New Zealand's greenhouse gas emissions.
In 2022, transport accounted for approximately 17.5% of New Zealand's total greenhouse gas emissions.
This makes transport an important area to focus on when looking for ways to reduce emissions.

How we travel matters

A large number of vehicles in New Zealand still rely on petrol or diesel. When these fuels are burned, they release carbon dioxide into the atmosphere.
This means the way we travel every day can contribute to New Zealand's carbon emissions.

But transport is also an area where we can make a change.
